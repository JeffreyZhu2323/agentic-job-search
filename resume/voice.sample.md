# Voice: human-writing spec + samples (public sample)

Public, fictional sample of the private `resume/voice.md`. The real file holds the same spec plus the candidate's actual writing samples (kept out of the public repo). The skills read `voice.md` before generating any candidate-facing prose so it reads like a specific person wrote it, not a model.

## The target
Write so it reads like the candidate wrote it, not like a model. Tune to the **optimal for the channel, not maximally casual**: overshooting reads unprofessional or strips the ATS keywords a recruiter's filter needs. The samples at the bottom anchor "their voice"; match that cadence, not a generic "human" average.

## Do
- Vary sentence length and rhythm. Uniform medium-length sentences are the clearest model tell; mix short and long.
- Use plain, strong words: "used" not "leveraged", "built" not "spearheaded", "large" not "robust".
- Lead with concrete specifics (the real thing done, real numbers and names) over abstraction.
- Let structure be a little asymmetric. Don't force every line into one "verb + noun + to + outcome" mold.
- Cut throat-clearing: "in order to", "responsible for", "it's worth noting", "I am reaching out to".

## Avoid the tells
- Buzzword stacking: leveraged, spearheaded, robust, seamless, passionate, results-driven, dynamic, synergy, cutting-edge, delve, "fast-paced world".
- Formulaic parallelism and the rule-of-three everywhere.
- The "not just X, but Y" construction.
- Inflated or grandiose voice; vague padded claims.
- Dash as a clause connector (em or hyphen). Use commas, colons, or separate sentences.

## Calibrate to the channel (optimal, not max)
- **Outreach, referral asks, emails:** warmest and most personal. Contractions fine. Specific to the person and the moment. Never templated; if it could be sent to anyone, it's wrong.
- **LinkedIn / profile About (careers-site bios):** warm and first-person, still professional. A real person talking about their work, not a brochure.
- **Resume bullets, work-experience and project descriptions:** "human" here means plain strong verbs and real specifics with the tells stripped. Stays tight, formal, and ATS-keyword-aware. No contractions or casual tone.

## Self-edit pass
Before finalizing anything, reread it once: cut the tells, confirm the rhythm varies, and check it sounds like the candidate at the right register for the channel. On resumes, keep it a light scan-and-fix on changed lines, not a rewrite.

## Examples (before / after)
- **Resume bullet.** AI: "Leveraged a robust architecture to seamlessly spearhead a 30% efficiency gain." Human: "Rebuilt the ingestion job that cut nightly batch time from 40 to 28 minutes."
- **Outreach opener.** AI: "I am reaching out to express my strong interest in leveraging my skills to contribute to your dynamic team." Human: "I saw the data role your team just posted and wanted to reach out. I spent last summer building fraud models on payments data, and it looks like a strong fit."
